# Rigorous Textual Review

A modular review discipline for long-form writing. Each pass below is a named unit the user can request on its own; a full audit runs them in order. Every finding should be specific (quote the text), actionable (supply replacement text or an exact fix), and calibrated (ranked by priority and effort).

## Operating principles

- Work from the raw source file when one exists: unzip .docx/.odt/.epub packages and inspect the XML directly for footnote integrity, formatting runs, comments, and tracked changes; text extraction renumbers notes, drops superscripts, and mangles italics, and each produces false findings.
- Diff each new version against the previous one; classify every change as fix, regression, or new material. Regressions (a corrected term reverting, a hedge removed, a modifier left dangling by a partial edit) are invisible to a fresh read and obvious in a diff.
- Keep a four-column ledger across rounds: resolved, open, deliberately kept by the author, newly introduced. Re-verify every prior finding each round by searching for the exact phrase; never assume an item persisted or was fixed.
- Do a clean-room read before consulting earlier notes, then reconcile, and report misses in both directions: what the fresh read caught that the notes lacked, and what the notes caught that the fresh read missed.
- State what was independently verified versus taken on trust. Re-derive load-bearing numbers with your own code rather than the author's; agreement then means something.
- Own errors explicitly, including tooling errors. Calibrate any sentence splitter or counter against italics, abbreviations, ellipses, and quotation marks before trusting its statistics, and keep the tool fixed across rounds so version comparisons are valid.
- When the author pushes back, separate concession of form from concession of substance, verify the counterclaim independently, find the single crux the disagreement turns on, state it in one sentence, and concede fully where wrong.
- Never re-litigate a settled author decision; list it once, without argument, as a last-call item so the final state is a decision rather than an oversight.
- After every round of applied suggestions, sweep for paste seams: doubled words, straight quotes, dangling modifiers from partial application, orphaned note anchors, dropped articles.
- Report only where a real gain exists; the absence of a suggestion is itself a verdict that a sentence should stand. Heightening every sentence flattens the peaks.
- Give drop-in replacement text, not abstract advice. Maintain a protect list of passages that must survive further editing.
- Rank findings with a priority score and an effort estimate, and say explicitly which items gate submission and which are refinements.
- Do not change a verdict without saying what new information changed it.

## Passes (what the user can ask for)

- Structural pass: outline, spine, excursi, handoffs, forward references, peak placement, body-versus-notes allocation.
- Flow pass: meanders, side branches, conclusions announced before derivation, duplicated arguments, unbridged movements.
- Logic and modality pass: inference gaps, hidden or interpretation-dependent premises, circularity, claim-class audit, superlatives, hedge placement.
- Accuracy pass: re-derive numbers, verify dates, attributions, quotations, and known misconceptions; report verified versus trusted.
- Terminology pass: field-collision words, named principles, first-use definitions, proper-noun spelling.
- Notes pass: integrity, density, condensing table with word targets and replacement text, promotion and demotion.
- Sentence-length pass: statistics plus a split table for every sentence over a threshold, with full replacement text.
- Register and beauty pass: diction, cliché, intensifiers, elevation devices, protect list.
- Breath map: intensity contour, trenches, cliffs, crowded summits, final cadence, with drop-in text.
- Style-guide pass: conformance to the named house style (punctuation with quotes, note-mark placement, dashes, hyphenation, numbers, capitalization, titles, citations, usage).
- Final polish: straight quotes, doubled words, spacing, formatting runs, comments and tracked changes, metadata, last-call list, submit verdict.
- Reception read: target-reader simulation, emotional and cognitive impact, milieu placement, objection and collision forecast, pull quotes.
- Companion-document fidelity check: a piece that compresses a technical source must claim no more than the source proves, and must keep the source's labels (axiom, postulate, conjecture) intact.
- Pushback adjudication: independent verification, crux identification, explicit concessions.
- Editor's cut: identify the most dispensable ten percent before an editor does.
- Clean-room full audit: fresh read, then reconcile against every prior ledger.

## Framing and front matter

- Title-to-scope match: read the title as a promise; check that late pivots (a change of discipline, a devotional or personal close) are covered by it.
- Subtitle or standfirst as orientation: it should name every major movement; an unannounced pivot at the sixty-percent mark reads as bait.
- Early orienting frame: if the first thesis-shaped sentence arrives paragraphs in, propose a two-sentence frame near the top.
- First sentence: does it reward attention, or front-load administrative detail? Compare it with the piece's best sentence.
- Metadata: byline, date, venue line, and document properties (author field, revision history, comments, tracked changes) current and appropriate for the submission type.
- Headings: present, in one consistent register, matched by each section's opening sentence, numbered under one scheme.
- Time-sensitive phrasing ("today," "in recent years") ages badly in print; suggest a dated or durable form.
- Headline claims must not exceed hedged body claims.

## Structure

- Produce a section-and-paragraph outline first; most structural findings fall out of seeing the shape.
- Identify the argumentative spine (the sections that must be read in order for the conclusion to follow); anything off the spine is a candidate for relocation or a note.
- Mark each paragraph's rhetorical mode (exposition, exegesis, argument, exhortation, narrative); flag switches without signposting.
- Detect excursi: a section that ends by telling the reader to set its contents aside is a parenthesis, not a step.
- Detect topic-jumping and side branches by listing each paragraph's topic; a section whose topics do not chain is meandering.
- Detect conclusions stated before they are derived; defer them or frame them as previews.
- Detect arguments duplicated across sections and points re-established later; note which location is the natural home; distinguish deliberate refrains from accidental repetition.
- Detect unbridged movements: two topics under one heading joined by a thin connective; strengthen the bridge or split.
- Read the last sentence of each section against the first of the next; the connection should be stated, not merely adjacent.
- Forward references: any concept or term used before it is introduced; fix by reordering or a one-clause preview.
- Section length variance: compute words per section; very short sections do not repay their heading, very long ones need internal breaks.
- Peak placement: the signature idea belongs at a summit (a section end, an emphasized thesis), not mid-paragraph.
- Cross-reference integrity: every "as we will see" or section pointer points where it says; reorderings break these silently.
- Bookends and echoes: check that an opening figure returning at the close actually closes.
- Consider splitting into companion pieces when independent theses exceed one piece's carrying capacity; name the division and its cost.
- Allocation: promote load-bearing content buried in notes; demote technical asides clogging the densest paragraphs.
- Cognitive-load budget: count the distinct new concepts introduced and check whether they arrive spaced or stacked; consider a glossary or sidebar for many terms or many dated claims.

## Argument and logic

- Write each inference chain as premises and conclusion; the missing step is usually visible only when written out.
- Circularity: a conclusion offered as its own reason; rewrite with the actual contradiction or evidence.
- Universal negatives and impossibility claims cannot be demonstrated; the evidential pattern usually argues better than the assertion.
- Disjunctions: when an argument establishes "A and/or B," check whether a later step quietly assumes B alone.
- Necessary versus sufficient conditions stated coherently (a sufficient condition needs no companions).
- Hidden premises contested in the relevant field: list them and check whether the piece names them.
- Interpretation-dependent premises: a mechanism resting on one reading of a theory should say so, or readers holding the rival reading dismiss the whole.
- Internal consistency between body and the author's own notes or companion work; a note that concedes what the body denies is a referee's gift.
- Cross-section tension: a claim localized in one section and generalized in another needs one reconciling sentence.
- Elimination arguments: are the enumerated alternatives exhaustive by stated axiom or by assertion? Make the axiom visible.
- Self-undermining arguments: a definitional-gap argument ("no one can define X") cuts against confident claims about X; make sure the conclusion rests on a different leg.
- Cumulative-case structures: test each link independently and name the weakest; hostile readers attack the weakest link and discount the cascade.
- Objection handling: does the piece name and answer its strongest objection? Naming costs little; ignoring invites the review that supplies it.
- Framing premises ("closed system," "all that exists," "any conceivable") hide scope commitments; make them explicit.
- Standard rebuttals to standard intuition pumps: if the piece uses a classic thought experiment, expect the classic reply and answer it.

## Claims and modality

- Classify every claim as theorem, empirical fact, axiom, postulate, conjecture, interpretation, or resonance, and check the verb matches ("proves," "shows," "suggests," "may," "lends resonance to"). A speculative section is watertight when every sentence declares its class correctly.
- Inflation audit: "can never," "no possible," "with certainty," "necessarily," "exactly zero"; supply the softening that loses no force.
- Superlatives audit: every "first," "only," "most," "never," "nowhere" checked for defensibility; superlatives are the sentences critics quote.
- Hedge stacking: one hedge is mystery, three is nervousness.
- Hedge placement: hedges belong on conjectures and are limp at summits; a thesis sentence with "could very reasonably be" drains its own peak.
- Speculation labeled once at the top of its section, with interior sentences honoring the label.
- Concordance and aging risk: when a text is read against a later framework, is the framework's stable core invoked or its frontier? Frontier-dependent claims belong in quarantine as conjecture.
- Retrofitting exposure: acknowledge interpretation when fitting an older text to a newer model; say how many readings were available.

## Accuracy

- Re-derive every checkable number: conversions, rates, ratios, exponents, travel times, orders of magnitude, date arithmetic. A factor-of-ten slip in a note is common and fatal to trust.
- Priority claims ("X years before Y") verified against both dates and stated identically in title, body, and notes.
- Historical attributions and coinages: who proposed, coined, or first observed; popular attributions are often wrong.
- Quotations: wording, locators, and who is actually speaking in the cited passage; named speakers imported from tradition but absent from the text are a conflation readers catch.
- Precision consistency: significant figures should match across mentions of the same quantity.
- Known popular misconceptions in the field; keep a list per discipline and check each against it.
- Evidence-type honesty: distinguish simulation, rendering, prediction, and observation.
- Example-to-claim fit: each illustration must illustrate the claim it accompanies.
- Statistical claims: sample sizes, confidence, base rates, effect sizes, survivorship.
- Units and dimensional sanity in equations and quantities.
- Links and references resolve; cited editions exist.

## Terminology

- Field-collision words (a term with different meanings in two disciplines) each get one disambiguating clause or note.
- Named principles and results: right person, right year, right form; a thesis is not a theorem, a conjecture is not a law.
- Definition at first use for every technical term; list terms in order of appearance to check.
- Opaque coinages the reader cannot decode: expand or cut.
- Folk use of a term that has a precise technical meaning: substitute the plain word.
- Metaphors that import a contested framework into an argument against that framework; flag the irony a reviewer will enjoy.
- Proper nouns and transliterations checked against a reference; these survive many rounds unnoticed.
- Abbreviations introduced once with the full form, then used consistently; watch for two forms of one name.

## Quotations and sources

- Separate the frame from the quotation; a long framing clause welded to a long quotation makes a monster sentence, and the quotation gains weight standing alone.
- Splice grammar: ellipses and bracketed insertions must yield a grammatical sentence when read aloud.
- Consecutive block quotations with no authorial voice between them flatten the piece; trim one or interpose a sentence.
- Quote the operative clause, not the passage; move the remainder to a note.
- Label the status of sources within a tradition (canonical, homiletic, private, folk); readers weight them differently.
- Sensitive or restricted material checked for appropriateness to venue and audience, and for conflation with public texts.
- Copyright and fair use for long non-public-domain quotations.
- Citation format consistency: abbreviation style, locator punctuation, edition, and terminal punctuation on citation notes.
- Scare-quote density: distancing quotation marks read as unconfident; remove or commit.

## Notes and apparatus

- Integrity in the raw file: definitions versus references, empties, orphans, duplicates.
- Density: notes per thousand body words and note wordage as a share of the whole; above roughly a third, the notes are a second essay.
- Classify each note as bare citation, essential argument, or digression; condense or cut digressions with word targets and replacement text.
- Merged notes (two topics, a missing paragraph break) split and re-anchored.
- Anchor placement: the mark sits where a reader would look, at the sentence that names the concept.
- Duplicate definitions across notes: merge, or make the second a pointer.
- Notes containing original research belong in their own piece or a labeled section.
- Identify the single most exposed note and make it bulletproof or explicitly speculative.
- The final note is read; it should end well.

## Comprehensibility

- State the target reader's expertise profile before judging; findings change with the profile.
- Concept-stacking: more than two or three new ideas per paragraph without a worked example loses the lay reader.
- Compression hot spots: a four-step inference in one sentence needs one intermediate sentence.
- Relatable anchors (the everyday illustration of an abstract effect) belong in the body, not a note.
- One governing metaphor per section, drawn from the piece's own image system.
- Mode signposting when the register changes; skip-ahead signposts for technical asides.
- Long parenthetical reference strings interrupt reading; move them to notes.
- Length against venue norms; prerequisites assumed versus the reader model.
- Belief-status calibration for persuasive or devotional writing: does the piece presume, invite, or argue for its stance, and does that match the venue's readership?
- Readability metrics only to locate outlier passages, never to judge.

## Prose and register

- Register dips (colloquialisms, corporate or engineering jargon, self-help vocabulary, cliché) and register spikes (archaic or period constructions); replace with in-register equivalents.
- Charity toward the milieu and the opposition: sneering at the past the piece elsewhere praises, or at the people it argues against, costs more than it earns.
- Word-frequency audit: intensifiers and pet words; a repeat within a sentence is an error, within a paragraph a tic, across the piece texture.
- Filler intensifiers ("simply," "very," "really," "truly," "in a deep sense"); deletion almost always strengthens.
- Latinate abstraction and nominalization where a concrete image or verb exists; preposition chains recast around the verb.
- Verb precision (near-synonyms with different meanings) and idiom errors.
- Personal-authority and credentials claims: cost versus benefit, and body versus note.
- Voice consistency: person, contractions, exclamation marks; singletons are conspicuous.
- Earned second person: one "you" at the moment of highest personal stake lands; scattered "you" is chatty.
- Passive-voice audit: convert where the agent matters, especially when the agent is the thesis.
- Adverb density and adverb-plus-abstraction pairs.
- Faulty parallelism in lists and tricolons; mixed metaphors within a sentence.
- Beauty-pass principles: concrete over abstract; cut the intensifier; end on the strong word; stay in the piece's own image system; most gains are subtractions.
- Elevation devices, sparingly: anaphora for inventories, tricolon for definitions, negative inversion for a final beat, the short lander after a long build.

## Rhythm and breath

- Sentence-length distribution: mean, median, share over 40 and over 50 words, share under 15; report the trajectory across versions with a fixed parser.
- Variance, not uniform shortening: after any sentence over 40 words, one under 15; the target is contrast.
- Three reliable split moves: frame from quotation; semicolon inventory into an anaphoric run; a "however/and" hinge into two sentences.
- Paragraph openers: a short orienting sentence before the heavy one.
- Section closers: the last sentence of each section is the most privileged rhythmic position; it should land, not trail.
- Discourse-marker frequency and sentence-opening variety (sentences beginning with "The," "This," "However").
- Rhetorical-question clustering and exclamation marks.
- Wall paragraphs: break at the thesis sentence; a paragraph break is the cheapest breath.
- Intensity contour: map emotional altitude section by section; find crowded summits (two emphasized theses adjacent), fused twin peaks (thesis and image in one sentence), technical trenches (long stretches with no human beat), and tonal cliffs between sections; fix with de-emphasis, a split, a surfacing sentence naming the reader's stake, or a descent sentence.
- Final cadence: the last word of the piece should be strong and warm; a prepositional knot as the last breath is the commonest failure.
- Semicolon and parenthesis density per thousand words as a signal of sentences that should be several.
- Notice self-referential effects (the piece describing the experience it induces) and protect them.

## Typography, formatting, and style-guide conformance

- Emphasis density: italic span count and words in italics; stacks of bold inside italics or capitals; reserve bold for at most one defined term.
- Formatting runs in the raw file: italics starting or ending on a space, straddling note marks, or containing only punctuation.
- Straight versus curly quotes and apostrophes, especially after pasting; fix globally by replacing each character with itself under smart-quote autocorrect.
- Dash ledger: em dashes counted separately inside and outside quotations; hyphen, en dash, em dash, and minus sign used correctly (ranges, compound names, exponents).
- Punctuation with closing quotation marks per the house style; note reference marks placed after punctuation (a macro can move them).
- Hyphenation per the house dictionary: adverb-plus-adjective compounds open, closed prefixes, hyphenated phrasal adjectives, compound nouns as listed.
- Numbers rule chosen and stated (spell-out threshold), fractions, ordinals, dates, decades, ranges with "from... to."
- Headline capitalization per the house style; title italics for books, films, periodicals; quotation marks for articles and poems; italics for isolated foreign words.
- Citation abbreviations consistent between parentheticals and notes; locator punctuation (space after commas in verse or page lists).
- Usage list of the house style (e.g., preferred variants of toward/towards, disfavored constructions such as "comprised of," Latin abbreviations restricted to parentheses with commas).
- Capitalization policy for institutional, doctrinal, or technical terms decided once and applied consistently; note where the house style and the tradition's own style diverge and ask the editor.
- Superscripts, math typesetting (italic variables, roman digits and units), Unicode hygiene (non-breaking spaces, ellipsis character versus spaced periods, multiplication and minus signs), serial comma, spelling variants.
- Comments, tracked changes, headers, footers, page numbers, fonts, hyperlinks, and figure or table captions checked before submission.

## Grammar sweep

- Fragments and comma splices; check each sentence for a main clause.
- Subject-verb agreement after long subjects and appositive lists.
- Dangling and misplaced modifiers, especially after restructuring.
- Verb-of-motion and preposition errors; ambiguous appositives and comma soup around parentheticals.
- Antecedent clarity for bare "this" and "it"; pronoun and joint-possessive consistency.
- Redundant doublets; doubled words and missing articles from paste seams.
- Tense consistency within passages.

## Reception and milieu

- Model the target reader explicitly (expertise, commitments, venue) and read as them.
- Emotional arc: where the guard drops, where intimacy lands, where whiplash or over-mechanization risk arises.
- Durable takeaways: the two or three ideas a reader will still hold a month later; check they are stated crisply enough to travel.
- Objections by discipline, ranked by force; write the first sentence of each field's likely review.
- Home-turf versus away premises: what the target audience already grants versus what is contested outside it.
- Lineage within the author's tradition: predecessors, revivals, departures, cautionary precedents.
- Nearest neighbors outside the tradition and the precise point of divergence from each; what is genuinely novel.
- Collision forecast: schools, movements, and rival readings the piece contradicts, intended or not; the sharpest responses come from there.
- Reader-segment forecasts: what each type will feel, object to, and do (share, cite, rebut).
- Venue fit: house style, typical length, argumentative versus contemplative expectation, note conventions.
- Cultural moment: which present anxieties the piece answers, deliberately or not.
- The sentence critics will quote (usually a universal negative or a superlative); pull-quote candidates; controversy budget spent on the claims that matter.

## Deliverable formats

- Findings tables with three columns: actual text, replacement text, reasoning. Quote the text exactly; make the replacement drop-in.
- Ranked tables with priority (1 to 10) and effort (S, M, L), separating gating items from refinements.
- Statistics blocks (sentence-length distribution, note density) with version-to-version trajectory.
- The round ledger (resolved, open, deliberately kept, new) and a protect list.
- A last-call list of author decisions, stated once, without argument.
- A closing verdict: what gates submission, what does not, and the estimated effort to close.

## Tooling notes

- Regex sweeps: straight quotes, doubled words, double spaces, adverb-plus-hyphen compounds, hyphens in numeric ranges, missing spaces after commas in locators, ellipsis spacing, prefix compounds, house-style usage words.
- Raw-package checks (for .docx and similar): note definitions versus references, empty or orphaned notes, comment and tracked-change parts, formatting run boundaries, metadata.
- Sentence statistics with a parser validated against the document's own quirks; keep it unchanged across rounds.
- Independent verification scripts for any load-bearing computation, written from scratch.
- Word-frequency counts for intensifiers, discourse markers, and pet words; per-section word counts; note word counts.
